If you need help right now

You are not alone. Help is available now.

Clarity is not an emergency service, and our WhatsApp line and booking form are not watched around the clock. If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, or thinking about ending their life, please contact one of these services now.

  • 999 · 112Police and ambulance emergency, anywhere in KenyaIf a life is in danger right now
  • 1199Kenya Red Cross, toll-freeVerified on redcross.or.ke
  • 0722 178 177Befrienders Kenya: emotional support and suicide prevention, by call or text+254 722 178 177
  • 0900 620 800Niskize: mental health and psychosocial support helplineListed by Clarity as 24/7
  • 0800 720 002Mental Health Kenya: toll-free advice and referralListed by Clarity
  • Nearest hospitalGo to the emergency department, or take the person there. Don't wait for an appointment.Treat it as a medical emergency

Outside Kenya? Call your local emergency number, for example 999 in the UK, 911 in the US and Canada, or 112 across the EU.

If someone you love is at risk

What you can do right now

  1. Stay with themDon't leave a person alone if you think they may act on thoughts of suicide.
  2. Ask directly and listenAsking “Are you thinking about ending your life?” does not put the idea in their head. Listen without judging or arguing.
  3. Make the space saferIf it's safe to do so, remove things they could use to hurt themselves, such as medicines, weapons or ropes.
  4. Call for help togetherCall Befrienders Kenya or Niskize with them, or take them to the nearest hospital. If there is immediate danger, call 999 or 112.
  5. Follow upOnce the crisis has passed, help them connect with ongoing support, such as a counsellor.

Adapted from Clarity's guide, How to help someone who is suicidal.

Warning signs

When to act straight away

  • Talking about wanting to die or being a burden
  • Looking for ways or means to end their life
  • Saying goodbye or giving away belongings
  • Sudden calm after a long period of distress
  • Withdrawing from family, friends and activities
  • Increased drinking or drug use

Getting help is care, not a crime. Kenya's High Court struck down the law that criminalised attempted suicide in January 2025. Taking someone to a hospital or a helpline is not a legal risk to them.

When the crisis has passed

Ongoing support at Clarity

Once you're safe, talking regularly with a counsellor can help you understand what happened and rebuild, at your own pace. Our counsellors also support families after a suicide loss.
