About our nurses

Who are your nurses, and are they qualified?

Our care team are trained nurses and midwives, and every nurse holds a current practising licence. Before care starts we tell you who is coming, and you are welcome to see their certificates and licence. Rachael Mugenyi leads the team and stays in touch with you throughout.

How do you check the people you send into my home?

Each nurse is interviewed in person, their documents and references are checked, and they carry a JoyfulHome ID. We introduce your nurse before the first shift (in person or by video call), and if the match doesn’t feel right, tell us and we will change the nurse.

What does the nurse do, and what don’t they do?

Your nurse cares for mother and baby: feeding support, bathing, cord and skin care, settling and safe sleep, comfort care after birth, watching for warning signs, and keeping written notes. They also keep baby’s things in order: bottles sterilised, baby laundry, a tidy nursery.

They don’t replace your doctor or midwife, don’t prescribe medicines, don’t deliver babies at home, and are not housekeepers for the whole household. If anything worries them, they tell you straight away and help you get to a health facility.

Do you only care for the baby?

No. We care for mothers too: in pregnancy, after birth (including after a C-section) and while breastfeeding. A rested, supported mother is at the heart of everything we do.

Booking & fees

How much does home care cost?

Our rates depend on the level of care and the hours of service tailored to your need. As a guide: home visits from UGX 60,000 (≈ US$16), day care from UGX 70,000 a day (≈ US$19), night care from UGX 90,000 a night (≈ US$24) and live-in care from UGX 550,000 a week (≈ US$150). These are sample starting rates for this preview. See plans & fees.

When should I book?

Ideally from around 30–34 weeks of pregnancy, so we can meet you, plan the hours and have your nurse ready when baby arrives. We also help families at short notice after birth: message us and we will tell you honestly what we can arrange.

How much notice do you need?

For a single home visit, usually 24–48 hours. For regular day, night or live-in care, about one week lets us match the right nurse. Same-day help is sometimes possible; ask on WhatsApp.

How do I pay?

By Mobile Money or bank transfer. For regular care we usually ask for the first week in advance, then weekly. You receive a written quote before anything is booked, and family abroad can pay for you.

I live abroad. Can I book care for my sister or daughter in Kampala?

Yes, and many families do. Book on WhatsApp, we agree the plan with you and the new mum, and we can send you short updates (with her permission). Prices are in Ugandan shillings; dollar amounts on this site are approximate.

During care

Can you care for twins or premature babies?

Yes. For twins we plan the hours (and sometimes a second nurse) around two feeding schedules. For babies who were born early or spent time in a newborn unit, we follow the hospital’s discharge plan and your paediatrician’s advice. Talk to us about your baby’s needs.

What does a live-in nurse need from us?

A safe, private place to sleep and rest, meals during their stay, and agreed time off each day. We put this in writing before care starts so everyone knows what to expect.

What happens in an emergency?

Your nurse is trained to spot warning signs in mother and baby. If something is wrong, they will tell you at once, advise you to go to your hospital or call your doctor, and help you get there. Please keep your hospital’s and doctor’s numbers on the fridge; we will ask for them when you book.

Which areas do you cover?

We serve families in Kampala and nearby parts of Wakiso, including Kololo, Ntinda, Bukoto, Muyenga, Kira, Najjera and Naalya. Message us with your location and we will confirm.
