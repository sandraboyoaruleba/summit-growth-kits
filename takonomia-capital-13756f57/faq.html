Buying land

What is a land search and why do I need one?

A land search at the land registry confirms who the registered owner is and whether there are mortgages, caveats or other claims on the title. It is the most important first step before paying for any registered land.

How much does an official land search cost?

The Ministry of Lands’ 2025 service schedule lists a registry search report at UGX 10,000 plus a UGX 5,000 perusal fee, and online searches on the Uganda National Land Information System portal at UGX 10,000 each. Our verification packages include these fees and add a site visit and written report.

Which tenure is safest to buy?

Each tenure system can be safe if it is checked properly. Freehold and registered mailo give ownership in perpetuity; mailo land may have lawful occupants with protected rights; leasehold runs for a fixed term. See our tenure guide.

What is a caveat?

A caveat is a notice on the title that someone claims an interest in the land. A title with a caveat cannot simply be transferred until the claim is resolved, so it must be checked before you pay.

How long does a land transfer take?

It depends on the land office and the documents. Plan for several weeks to a few months. We follow up until the title is in your name.

Buying from abroad

Can I buy land in Uganda while living abroad?

Yes. We handle searches, site visits and paperwork on your behalf, sending video walk-throughs and scanned documents at each step.

Can a non-citizen own land in Uganda?

Non-citizens may hold land on leasehold, not freehold or mailo. We explain what applies to you before you commit.

How do I pay safely from abroad?

Payments are staged and tied to verified steps and signed documents, and are made to accounts confirmed in the sale agreement. Never pay cash to a stranger on the strength of a photo of a title.
