What we check

A search is more than a stamp

The registry tells you what is on paper. We also check what is on the ground, because in Uganda the two do not always agree.

  • Registered owner & size

    Official search at the land registry or through the online land information portal.

  • Encumbrances

    Mortgages, caveats, court orders and other claims on the title.

  • Cadastral map

    We confirm the plot on the map record and compare it with the ground.

  • Boundaries

    A site visit with a surveyor to open or confirm boundary marks.

  • Occupants & neighbours

    We meet occupants, neighbours and local leaders about any claims.

  • Tenure

    Which of the four tenure systems applies, and what that means for you.

Land tenure, made clear

Uganda’s four tenure systems

Article 237 of Uganda’s Constitution recognises four ways of holding land. Knowing which one applies to a plot is the first step to a safe purchase.

I

Customary

Land held under the customs of a community. Often unregistered: a certificate of customary ownership can formalise it.

II

Freehold

Ownership in perpetuity, registered with a certificate of title. The most complete form of ownership.

III

Mailo

Registered ownership in perpetuity, common in Buganda, but lawful occupants on the land may have protected rights.

IV

Leasehold

Land held for a fixed period from a landlord, such as a kingdom, district land board or private owner, on agreed terms.

General information only (source: UN-Habitat, A Guide to Property Law in Uganda). Every plot is different. Ask us about a specific plot.
