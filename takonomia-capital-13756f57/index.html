Land tenure, made clear

Uganda’s four tenure systems

Uganda’s Constitution recognises four ways of holding land. Knowing which one applies to a plot is the first step to a safe purchase.

I

Customary

Land held under the customs of a community. Often unregistered — a certificate of customary ownership can formalise it.

II

Freehold

Ownership in perpetuity, registered with a certificate of title. The most complete form of ownership.

III

Mailo

Registered ownership in perpetuity, common in Buganda — but lawful occupants on the land may have protected rights.

IV

Leasehold

Land held for a fixed period from a landlord, such as a kingdom, district land board or private owner, on agreed terms.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask

What is a land search and why do I need one?

A land search at the land registry confirms who the registered owner is and whether there are mortgages, caveats or other claims on the title. It is the most important first step before paying for any registered land.

Can I buy land in Uganda while living abroad?

Yes. We handle searches, site visits and paperwork on your behalf, sending video walk-throughs and scanned documents at each step. Note that the law sets specific rules for non-citizens, who may hold land on leasehold — we will explain what applies to you.

What are your fees?

Fees depend on the service — a land search, brokerage, management or development. We confirm all fees in writing before any work begins.

Which areas do you cover?

We work across Kampala and the surrounding districts, and can arrange searches and site visits further afield.

Do you manage properties you didn’t sell?

Yes. We manage residential and commercial rental properties for owners, whether or not we were involved in the purchase.
