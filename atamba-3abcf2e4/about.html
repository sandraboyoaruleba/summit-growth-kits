About Atamba

A children’s doctor for the way children grow

Atamba is the practice of Dr. Caroline Nerima, a paediatrician and developmental paediatrician. She works with families and children with developmental delay and disability, and with the doctors, therapists and teachers around them.

Her story

From general paediatrics to developmental care

Dr. Caroline has spent her career with children and the people who love them. She worked in general paediatrics and in paediatric HIV care and treatment programmes, and then as a paediatrician at Children’s Medical Center in Kampala, where her work centred on the assessment and management of children with developmental delay and disability.

She went on to specialist clinical training in Developmental Paediatrics at Red Cross War Memorial Children’s Hospital in Cape Town, South Africa, one of Africa’s best-known children’s hospitals.

As she puts it, developmental paediatrics is “a new profession in Uganda”. Atamba exists so that Ugandan families have somewhere to turn when a child’s development worries them, without travelling abroad and without being told to “just wait and see”.

In the press

Sharing what she knows with parents

In August 2026, New Vision asked Dr. Caroline about breastfeeding, comfort and thumb sucking. Her words, as quoted:

“Breastfeeding offers more than nourishment. It strengthens emotional security and bonding, making children less likely to seek comfort elsewhere.”

Dr. Caroline Nerima, New Vision

“Parents can take comfort in knowing that both breastfeeding and thumb sucking are natural parts of early childhood development, each playing a different role in helping babies grow, feed and feel secure.”

Dr. Caroline Nerima, New Vision

Teaching others

Helping more children be seen early

Dr. Caroline also trains the people who see children every day. Health workers, Village Health Teams and teachers in Uganda have learned to spot and support developmental needs through programmes she has led, including the WHO–UNICEF Caregiver Skills Training and the Malawi Developmental Assessment Tool, a tool developed for African children.

Why “Atamba”?

Every practice name carries a story. Dr. Caroline will share what “Atamba” means to her, and to the families she works with, here.

What we can already say: Atamba is a small, personal practice. When you book, you see Dr. Caroline herself, and she stays with your family through the assessment, the plan and the reviews that follow.

Our promise to families

  • Unhurried appointments, so your child can settle and play
  • Findings explained in plain words, with time for your questions
  • A written summary you can share with school and therapists
  • Fees agreed before you come
  • Your child’s information kept private and shared only with your consent
