Support for children who develop differently

Conditions we support

Every child, seen as a whole

Many children have more than one need. You do not have to know the right word for your worry before you contact us.

Developmental delay

When a child reaches milestones later than expected, in one area or several.

How we help: Finding the cause where possible, a plan for each area, and early therapy referrals.

Autism

Differences in communication, social connection, play and sensory experience, often with strong routines.

How we help: Careful diagnosis, a home and school plan, caregiver coaching and therapy links.

Cerebral palsy & neurodisability

Conditions affecting movement and posture, often with other needs such as epilepsy, feeding or learning.

How we help: Medical review, coordinated care with physiotherapy and others, and support for school.

ADHD, learning & behaviour

Trouble with attention, activity, impulsiveness, reading, writing or numbers, despite real effort.

How we help: Understanding the reasons, guidance for parents and teachers, and treatment options explained.

Speech & language delay

When words come slowly, speech is hard to understand, or a child struggles to follow what is said.

How we help: Finding out why (hearing, development, autism or other causes) and linking you with speech therapy.

Genetic & complex conditions

Conditions such as Down syndrome, and children with several needs at once, including epilepsy.

How we help: A doctor who sees the whole child and helps coordinate the many people involved.

Is my child on track?

Signs to watch, age by age

Children grow at different speeds, so use this as a guide only. If something on the right-hand list sounds familiar, or you simply have a feeling, it is okay to ask.

2 months

Most children

  • Smiles at people
  • Looks at faces, follows you with eyes
  • Lifts head during tummy time

Talk to us if

  • No response to loud sounds
  • Does not watch things as they move

6 months

Most children

  • Laughs, makes sounds back to you
  • Rolls over, reaches for toys
  • Knows familiar faces

Talk to us if

  • Very stiff or very floppy
  • No sounds or smiles in reply

12 months

Most children

  • Babbles “mama”, “dada”
  • Pulls to stand, may walk holding on
  • Waves bye-bye, points

Talk to us if

  • Not sitting by 9–10 months
  • No babbling or gestures

18 months

Most children

  • Says several words
  • Walks alone
  • Points to show you things

Talk to us if

  • No words, does not point
  • Loses words or skills they had

2 years

Most children

  • Puts two words together
  • Copies others, plays pretend
  • Runs, kicks a ball

Talk to us if

  • Not using two-word phrases
  • Does not copy actions or words

3 years

Most children

  • Talks in short sentences
  • Plays with other children
  • Strangers understand some speech

Talk to us if

  • Speech very hard to understand
  • Little interest in other children

4–5 years

Most children

  • Tells a simple story
  • Draws a person, holds a crayon well
  • Follows rules in games

Talk to us if

  • Cannot follow simple instructions
  • Very big, frequent meltdowns

School age

Most children

  • Reads and writes for their age
  • Makes and keeps friends
  • Pays attention for school tasks

Talk to us if

  • Falling behind despite effort
  • Repeated concerns from teachers

At any age: if your child loses skills they once had (words, walking, play), please seek advice promptly. These lists are a general guide based on widely used milestone checklists, not a diagnosis. For babies born early, use their corrected age.

Gentle truths

Things parents are often told, and what we know

“He’ll grow out of it.”

Some children do catch up, but many need support, and the brain learns fastest in the early years. Checking early never harms.

“It’s the mother’s fault.”

Developmental conditions are not caused by anything a parent did or did not do. No shame, no blame: just support.

“Boys just talk late.”

Children vary, but a toddler with very few words at two deserves a check, boy or girl.

“Nothing can be done.”

Every child can make progress. The right plan, therapy and support at home and school change what is possible.

When to come

You don’t need to be sure. You just need to ask.

  • You, a relative or a teacher feel something isn’t quite right
  • Your child was born early, or was very sick as a newborn
  • Your doctor has suggested a developmental review
  • Your child has a diagnosis and you want a clear plan
  • You are worried about school, behaviour or friendships
