Answers you can understand, and a plan you can use

A developmental assessment is a careful, play-based look at how your child moves, communicates, learns, plays and copes with daily life. You stay beside your child the whole time.

What we look at

Five areas of your child’s development

Development is more than one milestone. Dr. Caroline looks at the whole child, because the areas affect each other.

Movement

Big movements like sitting, walking and balance, and fine skills like holding a crayon or doing buttons.

Communication

Understanding, speaking, gestures and eye contact: how your child shares what they want and feel.

Thinking & learning

Play, problem-solving, attention and, for older children, reading, writing and numbers.

Social & play

How your child plays, shares attention, makes friends and responds to other people.

Behaviour & daily life

Sleep, feeding, toileting, routines and big feelings, and what makes daily life hard or easier.

Health & history

Pregnancy, birth, illnesses, hearing and vision, family history: the medical picture behind development.

Your assessment visit

An unhurried 90 minutes

Children do their best when they feel safe. The visit is paced around your child, with breaks whenever they need them.

Puzzles and simple games help us see how a child thinks and solves problems.

  1. 0 min

    Arrive and settle

    Your child explores the toys while you meet Dr. Caroline. No rush.

  2. 10 min

    Your story

    Pregnancy, birth, milestones, health, family and what you have noticed. Bring any reports.

  3. 35 min

    Play and observe

    Blocks, puzzles, books and pretend play. Dr. Caroline watches movement, language, attention and play.

  4. 60 min

    Health check

    A gentle physical and neurological examination, with your child on your lap if they prefer.

  5. 75 min

    Talking it through

    What we found, what it means, and what we suggest, in plain language. Your questions answered.

  6. Within a week

    Your written summary

    A report with the plan and any referrals, to share with your school and therapists if you wish.

Helpful to bring

  • Child health card / immunisation book
  • Previous reports or scans
  • School reports or teacher notes
  • Short phone videos of what worries you
  • A favourite toy or snack

What you leave with

Clarity, and a way forward

A written summary

What was assessed, what we found and what it means, in words you can share.

A practical plan

Two or three priorities for home, and what each person can do.

The right referrals

Speech, occupational or physiotherapy, hearing, vision or tests, only where needed.

A follow-up date

A review to see progress and adjust the plan together.

Recognised methods

Careful, evidence-based assessment

Dr. Caroline uses recognised developmental assessment tools alongside careful history-taking and observation. She is a Master Instructor for the Malawi Developmental Assessment Tool (MDAT), which was developed for African children, so results make sense in our context.

A diagnosis is never rushed. When it is clear, we tell you honestly and kindly. When it needs more time, we explain why, and what we will look for next.
