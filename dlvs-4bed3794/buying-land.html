Before you pay, check the land

Most land disputes start with a purchase nobody checked. DLVS walks the land, compares it with the documents and tells you plainly what you are buying.

01 The buyer’s checklist

Six checks before any money moves

General guidance for buyers in Uganda. Your lawyer handles the legal transfer; we handle the land on the ground and its value.

  1. 01

    Search the title

    Confirm at the land registry that the title is genuine, who the registered owner is, and whether there are caveats, mortgages or other encumbrances.

  2. 02

    Open the boundaries

    A registered surveyor finds the marks on the ground and confirms the land you are shown is the land on the title.

  3. 03

    Meet the neighbours and the LC

    Ask who has used the land, whether anyone else claims it, and whether there are graves, occupants or bibanja holders.

  4. 04

    Check for occupants and claims

    On mailo land especially, people already living or farming on the land may have rights that come with it.

  5. 05

    Get an independent valuation

    Know what the land is really worth before you agree a price, so you neither overpay nor undersell.

  6. 06

    Use a lawyer for the agreement

    A lawyer drafts the sale agreement and handles the transfer. We work alongside your lawyer, not instead of one.

02 For the diaspora

Buying from abroad? Be our eyes on the ground.

Many land frauds target buyers who cannot visit. We go for you, and report back in a way you can check from anywhere.

  • Site visit with GPS coordinates you can open on Google Maps.
  • Dated photos and a short video of the land, the boundaries and the neighbours.
  • A written summary of what we found on the ground versus the documents.
  • A valuation if you want one, before you agree the price.
  • WhatsApp and email updates in your time zone.

03 Warning signs

When to slow down

  • The seller is in a hurry and wants cash before any search.
  • The boundaries “will be shown later”.
  • The price is far below what nearby plots sell for.
  • Neighbours or the LC have not heard of the sale.
  • The title is a photocopy and the original “is with someone”.
  • The surveyor offered cannot show registration details.

04 Land tenure in Uganda

Four kinds of land tenure

A short, general guide. Ask your lawyer about your specific land.

Mailo

Mailo

Owned in perpetuity, mostly in Buganda. Lawful occupants (bibanja holders) may also have rights on the land.

Freehold

Freehold

Owned forever with a registered title. Common in many parts of the country.

Leasehold

Leasehold

Held for a set number of years from a landlord, often a district land board or the Kabaka’s land office.

Customary

Customary

Held under the customs of a community. A certificate of customary ownership can be issued.
