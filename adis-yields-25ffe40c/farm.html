A day on the farm

Every day, the same care

A typical day at Adis Yields. Times are a guide; the hens set the pace.

  1. 6:30am

    Feed & water

    Fresh layer feed and clean water for the whole flock before anything else.

  2. 8:00am

    First collection

    Eggs gathered from the nests by hand, gently, into clean trays.

  3. 9:30am

    Sort & check

    Every egg checked: cracked, misshapen or dirty eggs come out before crating.

  4. 11:00am

    Crate & store

    Good eggs go into 30-egg crates, kept cool and out of the sun.

  5. Midday

    Orders packed

    Standing orders are reserved first, then the day’s new WhatsApp orders.

  6. 2:00pm

    Second collection

    Afternoon eggs collected and sorted the same way.

  7. Afternoon

    Deliveries

    Crates leave for shops, schools and homes on their delivery day.

  8. Evening

    Houses checked

    Water topped up, houses closed and the flock settled for the night.

Hen welfare

Healthy hens lay better eggs

Good eggs start long before collection. This is how we look after the flock.

Balanced layer feed

A complete layer ration with the calcium hens need for strong shells.

Clean water, every day

Drinkers cleaned and refilled daily. Hens drink a lot, especially in the heat.

Clean, airy houses

Dry litter, good ventilation and enough space for every bird.

Biosecurity

Visitors by appointment only and footbaths at the door, to keep disease out.

Health checks

Vaccinations on schedule and a vet called whenever a bird looks unwell.

Calm routine

Same times, same people, gentle handling: calm hens lay steadily.

Quality check

What goes into your crate

Every egg is looked at before it is crated. Here is what we check.

In the crate

  • Clean, whole shells
  • Normal shape and size
  • Laid in the last few days

Taken out

  • Cracked or hairline-cracked
  • Dirty or stained
  • Odd-shaped or very thin shells

Egg care at home and in the shop

Keep your eggs fresh for longer

General good practice for storing eggs in Uganda’s warm climate.

Cool and shaded

Keep crates in a cool, dry place away from direct sun, cooking fires and walls that heat up.

Pointed end down

Store eggs pointed end down; it keeps the yolk centred and the egg fresher.

Don’t wash until use

Washing removes the egg’s natural protective layer. Wipe if needed, wash just before cooking.

First in, first out

In a shop or school store, sell or use the older crates first.

The water test

Fresh eggs sink and lie flat in a bowl of water. An egg that floats should be thrown away.

Fridge if you can

Refrigerated eggs keep even longer. Once chilled, keep them chilled.

General guidance, not a guarantee of shelf life. Ask us if you’re unsure about a crate.
