Buying land in Uganda: checks before you pay

General information, not legal advice. Every situation is different, so speak to an advocate before you act.

Ask ASP about this
  1. Get a copy of the title and run an official search at the land registry to confirm who the registered owner is and whether there are mortgages, caveats or other claims.
  2. Check the seller's identity against the title, and ask how they came to own the land.
  3. Visit the land yourself. Walk the boundaries, speak to neighbours and the local council (LC1) chairperson, and check who is actually living on or using it.
  4. If the land is family land, make sure any spouse whose consent the law requires has given it in writing.
  5. Ask a surveyor to confirm the boundaries match the title and the land you were shown.
  6. Put the deal in a written sale agreement, signed before witnesses, with payment terms tied to clear steps.
  7. Pay through traceable channels (bank or mobile money), never large amounts in cash.
  8. Complete the transfer at the land registry promptly, with the signed transfer forms and the stamp duty and fees paid, and keep copies of everything.

Starting a company with partners: agree it in writing

General information, not legal advice. Every situation is different, so speak to an advocate before you act.

Ask ASP about this
  1. Choose the right structure (sole proprietor, partnership or private limited company) for your risk, tax and growth plans.
  2. Agree who owns what share, and what each founder contributes: money, skills, time or assets.
  3. Write down who decides what, and what needs everyone's agreement (for example new shareholders, big loans or selling the business).
  4. Agree what happens if a founder leaves, falls ill or dies, and how their shares are valued and bought.
  5. Keep company money separate from personal money from day one, with clear records.
  6. Diary your annual compliance: returns, tax filings and licence renewals.
  7. Sign a shareholders' agreement now, while everyone still agrees.

Employing staff: the paperwork that protects you both

General information, not legal advice. Every situation is different, so speak to an advocate before you act.

Ask ASP about this
  1. Give every employee a written contract setting out the role, pay, hours, leave and notice.
  2. Keep a simple personnel file: ID, contract, job description, appraisals and any warnings.
  3. Write down your key policies: conduct, leave, use of company property, and how complaints are handled.
  4. Handle discipline fairly: explain the concern in writing, give the employee a chance to respond, and record the outcome.
  5. Before ending employment, take advice. A fair process now is cheaper than a dispute later.
  6. Make statutory deductions and remittances on time, and keep the receipts.

When a loved one dies: first steps for the family

General information, not legal advice. Every situation is different, so speak to an advocate before you act.

Ask ASP about this
  1. Obtain the death certificate. Most later steps depend on it.
  2. Look for a will: at home, with the family's advocate, at the bank or with trusted friends.
  3. Make a simple list of what the person owned and owed: land, accounts, vehicles, shares, debts.
  4. Keep property safe. Avoid selling or dividing anything until someone is legally authorised to act.
  5. Agree as a family who will apply to administer the estate, and seek advice on probate or letters of administration.
  6. Keep records of every payment made from the estate and every decision taken.

Common mistakes

Three things we wish every client did first

Put it in writing

Verbal promises are hard to prove. A short written agreement, signed and dated, prevents most disputes we see.

Check before you pay

Searches and due diligence cost little compared with the money at stake. Do them before, not after.

Act early

Deadlines in law are real. A letter in time often avoids a case later.
